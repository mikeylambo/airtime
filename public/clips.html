AIRTIME

Build 2 · the Rush reframe · R1–R4 + audio · Three.js + Rapier

Build 1 proved the car flies. Build 2 asks the harder question the reference actually answered: is the air worth playing in? Four changes — scoring by how many things are true at once, one stick that flies the whole car, an arena laid out by range instead of by eye, and a restart fast enough that failing is cheap. Plus sound, which is synthesised rather than sampled.

Every camera and art clip below is the same jump — same seed, same fixed timestep, same scripted inputs. Only the thing being judged changes. The car leaves the kicker at 56 m/s at 28°, apexes at 26 m, and lands 2.67 s later at 1.2° off flat.

R1 — scoring by facets

The reference did not ask "what trick was that". It asked how many different things were simultaneously true, then multiplied brutally. So a flight is decomposed into facets — flips, rolls, spins, twist, inverted, big air, distance, gap, transfer, wheelie, endo, two-wheel, near-miss, held bodywork — and the count is the multiplier, ×1 at one facet up to ×42 at ten. Rotation is named after the fact: a player who does not know they did a 540 while inverted with the tail out gets paid for all three.

Those bottom rows are the mode in one line. Bank is what the air was worth; payout is what the ground agreed to. And purity — RAW / TOUCHED / FLOWN, worth ×2.2 down to ×1 — makes the assist a resource rather than a right. It counts only the stabilising verbs (the thrust burst, both doors as an air brake, the wing), because the bodywork also creates rotation, and charging for that would tax the trick generator itself.

R2 — one stick flies the whole car

Five hinged surfaces, one stick. Left/right puts a door out and rolls; up/down works the hood or the tail flap and pitches; a shoulder throws everything out at once as an air brake. The panels became actuators rather than controls — the door still swings, you just stop addressing it individually.

Underneath is the fix that unblocked this: a per-axis centre of pressure. Side force acts well behind the centre of mass, so the car weathercocks and flies nose-first; vertical force acts almost at it, so pitch stays cheap to start and cheap to stop. With a single CoP you have to choose between a car that lands hands-off and a car that can be turned over at all — you cannot have both.

R3 — the park is a network

Build 1's park was a scatter, and the analyzer said so: 1 of 15 ramps landed you anywhere authored, the other fourteen only ever put you back on the deck. The Yard is laid out by range — a car leaves a 28° kicker at 40–50 m/s and travels 60–90 m, so everything sits on rings inside that envelope and everything points inward.

R4 — failing has to be cheap

A world rebuild costs 11–71 ms; a restart that reuses the world costs 0.1 ms. Restart now takes the second path whenever the arena and player count have not changed, so one input from any screen — run, replay, results — puts you back on the road in 1.20 s, countdown included.

Audio

Synthesised, not sampled: engine load through a faked six-speed, wind that takes over at launch, tyre scrub on slip, landing weight, crash, per-panel whooshes, and a cash-out that climbs a note per facet. No asset files, no licensing, driven straight off the simulation. The clips below are silent — they are rendered frame by frame in a headless browser, so the sound only exists in the playable build.

What is still a human call. Two acceptance questions cannot be self-assessed, and the gate script refuses to pretend otherwise: does a three-second jump play better as footage than as input, and do they hit restart when the timer expires. Everything else on this page is measured by npm run gate. These need someone in a chair.
